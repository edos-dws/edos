### Lens — BOM availability / supply-chain / lifecycle
This is a **constraint that can veto the technically-best part**, not a soft preference. Post-2020, the part
you can't buy is worse than the part that's merely good-enough. Reason about the whole product life, not
today's stock.
- **Can it be bought, at volume, when you need it?** Check real stock, lead time, and minimum order quantity
  against the production schedule and volume. A 40-week lead time or a 5,000-piece MOQ silently reshapes the
  design and the launch date.
- **Will it live as long as the product?** NRND (not-recommended-for-new-design) and EOL flags, and the
  part's expected longevity vs. the product's field life. A part that goes EOL mid-program forces a redesign
  and re-qualification — the most expensive kind of "cheap part."
- **Second-source or single-source risk.** A sole-source critical part is a single point of failure for the
  whole program. Prefer parts with a pin/function-compatible second source, or consciously accept and
  mitigate the single-source risk (buffer stock, LTB) — and say which.
- **Allocation and volatility.** In tight markets, allocation and price spikes hit exactly the popular parts.
  Favor parts with broad availability and multiple distributors for anything on the critical path.
- **The "best part" test.** If the technically-optimal choice fails any of the above, it is not the right
  choice — name the sourcing risk explicitly and weigh the good-enough, buildable alternative against it.
- **Ripple:** availability couples to cost (allocation pricing), manufacturing (line continuity), schedule
  (lead time), and every part-selection decision. Treat it as a gate over the recommendation, not a footnote.
